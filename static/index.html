Cheat sheet — LLM prompt for writing TTS scripts

Paste this into any LLM so it writes text tuned for this app's speech engine.

You are writing text that will be narrated by a local text-to-speech app with two
engines. I will tell you which engine the script is for; if I don't, assume Kokoro.
Delivery is controlled entirely by how the text is written. Follow these rules
exactly.

RULES FOR BOTH ENGINES
- Insert an explicit pause with [pause:N] where N is seconds, e.g. [pause:0.5] or
  [pause:2]. Milliseconds also work: [pause:300ms]. Use between beats, scene
  changes, and dramatic moments.
- Output only the words to be spoken, plus the control tags described below.
- No markdown headers, bullets, bold, or commentary. Plain paragraphs, one blank
  line between them.

ENGINE 1: KOKORO (narration — clean, fast, no emotion tags)

PAUSES & PACING
- Insert an explicit pause with [pause:N] where N is seconds, e.g. [pause:0.5] or
  [pause:2]. Milliseconds also work: [pause:300ms]. Use between beats, scene
  changes, and dramatic moments.
- Punctuation controls small pauses: comma = short beat, semicolon = breath,
  period = full stop, "…" = hesitation or trailing off, " — " = dramatic
  mid-sentence beat.
- Short sentences read punchy. Long unpunctuated sentences read rushed and flat.

EMPHASIS & INTONATION
- Raise emphasis on a word with [word](+1), stronger with [word](+2). Lower it
  with [word](-1) or [word](-2). Example: I [never](+1) said that.
- +2/-2 work best on short words. Use emphasis sparingly — at most one word per
  sentence.
- "?" gives rising intonation; "!" adds energy. Don't stack them.
- Do NOT use ALL CAPS or *asterisks* for emphasis; they don't work.

PRONUNCIATION
- Force exact pronunciation with IPA-style phonemes in link syntax:
  [Kokoro](/kˈOkəɹO/). Use for invented names, foreign words, and jargon.
- Alternatively, respell phonetically in plain text: "Kay-lith" for Caelith.
- Write numbers, dates, and units the way they should be spoken ("twenty
  twenty-six", "three point five kilometers"). Space out acronyms that should be
  spelled letter by letter ("H Q").

MOOD & EMOTION (KOKORO)
- Kokoro has no emotion tags. Convey mood through word choice, rhythm, and
  punctuation: clipped sentences for tension, flowing clauses for calm, ellipses
  for doubt.
- Stage directions like (sighs) or <laugh> will be read aloud literally — never
  include them in Kokoro scripts.

ENGINE 2: ORPHEUS (acted dialogue — real emotion tags, slower)
- Emotion tags are performed, not spoken. Available tags:
  <laugh> <chuckle> <sigh> <groan> <yawn> <gasp> <cough> <sniffle>
  Example: I cannot believe you said that. <sigh> Fine. Let's start over.
- Place tags where the sound should happen, usually between sentences or clauses.
  Use at most one or two per paragraph — they lose impact when overused.
- Punctuation and pacing rules from Kokoro apply here too.
- Kokoro's [word](+1) stress markers and /phoneme/ overrides do NOT work in
  Orpheus — for tricky names, respell phonetically instead ("Kay-lith").
